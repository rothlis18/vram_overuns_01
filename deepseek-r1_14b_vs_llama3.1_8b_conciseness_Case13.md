

The domestic economy, with 83.4% dollarization, faces a critical scenario when commercial banks invest $10.5 billion in offshore assets. Assuming a 20% reserve requirement, banks typically hold $20 billion in reserves for $100 billion in deposits. Investing $10.5 billion offshore reduces available reserves to $19.5 billion. If local depositors withdraw more than $19.5 billion, banks cannot meet obligations, triggering a liquidity crunch. Offshore shadow lenders, holding frozen funds, exert control, while the domestic system's reliance on these offshore assets leaves it vulnerable. The interplay of high dollarization, reduced reserves, and frozen offshore liquidity leads to a direct systemic risk, with power dynamics favoring offshore lenders and domestic institutions struggling to maintain stability.
