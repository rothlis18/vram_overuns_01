

The scenario involves a domestic economy heavily reliant on the US dollar (83.4% dollarized) facing a critical financial crisis due to $10.5 billion invested by commercial banks in offshore assets, coinciding with global shadow banking liquidity issues. Here's a structured analysis:

1. **Investment and Liquidity Issues**:
   - **Investment**: Commercial banks invest $10.5 billion in offshore assets for higher returns.
   - **Liquidity Crisis**: The global shadow banking network, which holds these assets, faces severe liquidity constraints, making it difficult to retrieve funds.

2. **Domestic Economy's Vulnerability**:
   - **High Dollarization**: 83.4% of financial transactions are in US dollars, creating significant exposure to global dollar dynamics.
   - **Reserve Freeze**: Institutional reserves are legally frozen in offshore vehicles, rendering them inaccessible during a liquidity crunch.

3. **Liquidity Freeze Mechanism**:
   - **Withdrawals and Reserve Requirements**: With a 20% reserve requirement, banks must hold $20 billion for $100 billion deposits. The $10.5 billion invested offshore reduces available reserves.
   - **Failure Threshold**: If more than $9.5 billion (remaining after freezing) is withdrawn, banks cannot meet demands, leading to potential failure.

4. **Power Dynamics**:
   - **Offshore Lenders' Control**: Shadow lenders hold the frozen funds, exerting control over domestic liquidity.
   - **Domestic Dependency**: The domestic system's reliance on offshore funds leaves it vulnerable, despite its capital autarky.

5. **Economic Impact**:
   - **Potential Domino Effect**: Bank failures can cascade, affecting the broader economy.
   - **Dollarization Risks**: A dollar shortage or depreciation exacerbates the crisis, forcing asset sales and further instability.

In conclusion, the interplay of high dollarization, frozen reserves, and offshore liquidity issues creates a precarious situation where domestic banks face insolvency upon significant withdrawals. The offshore shadow lenders' control over funds underscores the systemic risk and potential economic instability.
