**Forensic Systems-Engineering Analysis**

The scenario unfolds as follows: private commercial banks inject $10.5 billion into offshore assets, concurrently with the global shadow banking network's liquidity crisis. This synchronized event triggers a chain reaction, where local depositors withdraw cash, exacerbating the liquidity freeze. The banks' reserves, frozen offshore in distressed private credit vehicles, cannot be accessed to meet domestic depositors' demands.

**Liquidity Gap (ΔL)**

The liquidity gap arises from the difference between required domestic deposits (ΔD) and available reserves (ΔR). Assuming an 83.4% dollarization rate, the required domestic deposits can be estimated as:

ΔD = 0.834 \* Total Domestic Deposits

Given the total domestic deposits are not provided, we'll assume a hypothetical scenario for illustrative purposes. Let's assume a total domestic deposit of $125 billion (a rough estimate, as the actual figure is not provided). Then:

ΔD = 0.834 \* $125 billion ≈ $104.25 billion

The available reserves (ΔR) are frozen offshore, estimated at $10.5 billion. The liquidity gap (ΔL) is the difference between ΔD and ΔR:

ΔL = ΔD - ΔR ≈ $104.25 billion - $10.5 billion ≈ $93.75 billion

However, considering the initial $10.5 billion investment, the actual liquidity gap could be significantly higher, potentially exceeding $105 billion.

**Power Dynamics**

The offshore shadow lenders gain control over the domestic economy, dictating debt terms and credit availability. This power dynamic is rooted in the banks' reliance on offshore reserves, which are now frozen. The shadow lenders can manipulate the credit market, imposing harsh conditions on domestic borrowers. This could lead to a credit crunch, exacerbating the liquidity freeze and economic contraction.

**Systemic Fallout**

The resulting liquidity freeze and credit crunch could lead to a 10-20% GDP contraction, highlighting the severe economic risks posed by this situation. The power dynamics between offshore shadow lenders and domestic capital autarky are characterized by a stark asymmetry, where the former holds significant leverage over the latter. This asymmetry can lead to a loss of economic sovereignty, as domestic policymakers struggle to respond to the crisis.
