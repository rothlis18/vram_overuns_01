This action increases the risk of insolvency for domestic banks. This situation leads to "hard redemption gates," effectively preventing investors from retrieving their funds.

The domestic banking system is particularly vulnerable due to the significant amount of funds ($10.5 billion) frozen offshore. This reduction in available liquidity makes it challenging for banks to meet local withdrawal demands. Given the high dollarization rate of 83.4%, where local depositors predominantly hold their funds in foreign currencies, the potential inability of banks to provide cash exacerbates the risk of a bank run and subsequent economic instability.

The power dynamics in this scenario are heavily skewed towards offshore shadow lenders. These entities control a substantial portion of the liquidity, and their management of these funds directly impacts the domestic banking system's stability. The dependency of domestic banks on offshore lenders for liquidity management highlights the systemic risk posed by the struggles within the shadow banking network.

**Conclusion:**

The deployment of $10.5 billion into offshore assets by domestic banks, coupled with the global shadow banking network's liquidity issues, creates a highly volatile financial environment.
