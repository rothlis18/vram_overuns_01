When private commercial banks deploy $10.5 billion into offshore net foreign assets, they reduce the amount of liquid assets available for domestic economic activities. **Quantitative Impact:**

1. **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR):** The deployment of $10.5 billion offshore reduces the domestic banking system's LCR, making it more challenging for banks to meet short-term liquidity needs. If we assume an average LCR of 100% before the deployment, a 10.5% reduction in liquid assets would lower the LCR to 90.5%.

2. **Loan-to-Deposit Ratio (LDR):** With reduced liquid assets, banks may be forced to tighten lending standards or increase interest rates to attract depositors, thereby increasing the LDR. If the LDR was previously at 80%, a 10.5% reduction in liquid assets could push the LDR up to 88.8%.

**Interbank Network Analysis:**

The flow of funds between domestic and offshore institutions is governed by complex interbank lending agreements and regulatory frameworks. When offshore lenders face liquidity constraints, they may not be able to provide the necessary liquidity to domestic banks, creating a chokepoint in the interbank network. This can lead to a credit crunch, where domestic banks struggle to access short-term funding.

**Legal & Regulatory Constraints:**

The legal barriers preventing access to frozen reserves are rooted in jurisdictional complexities and regulatory differences between offshore private credit vehicles and domestic banking systems. When institutional reserves are frozen, banks may not be able to access these funds to meet withdrawal requests from depositors. Specific legal constraints include:

- **Cross-border Regulatory Differences:** Offshore private credit vehicles may be subject to different regulatory requirements than domestic banks, making it challenging to transfer funds across borders.
- **Freeze Mechanisms:** Legal provisions within these vehicles may include freeze mechanisms that prevent the transfer of funds, even in cases where the funds are needed to meet domestic withdrawal demands.

**Physical Infrastructure:**

The physical cash distribution networks play a critical role in the liquidity freeze. When banks face liquidity constraints, they may limit or suspend cash withdrawals, leading to operational constraints within the banking system. The capacity of these networks is often strain-tested during such events, and any reduction in liquid assets can exacerbate these limitations.

In conclusion, the deployment of $10.5 billion into offshore net foreign assets, coupled with the legal freezing of institutional reserves, creates a complex web of liquidity constraints and operational limitations. The resulting systemic fallout in a dollarized economy can be quantitatively measured through key ratios such as LCR and LDR, and is underpinned by the intricate dynamics of interbank lending, regulatory frameworks, and physical cash distribution networks.
