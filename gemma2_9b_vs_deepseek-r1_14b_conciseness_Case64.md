A $10.5 billion outflow from domestic banks to offshore assets, coinciding with a global shadow banking liquidity crisis, creates a direct chain reaction. 

Domestic banks, already depleted by capital flight, face immediate liquidity stress when depositors demand physical cash.  This demand is amplified by the 83.4% dollarization of the economy, meaning a large portion of financial activity relies on US dollars.  

The offshore private credit vehicles holding a portion of the $10.5 billion are experiencing redemption gates, meaning they cannot meet withdrawal demands. This creates a feedback loop: domestic banks cannot access their frozen reserves, leading to a liquidity freeze. Depositors, sensing instability, withdraw further, exacerbating the crisis.

The power dynamic shifts decisively towards offshore shadow lenders. They control the frozen assets, effectively holding the domestic economy hostage.  Domestic banks are rendered incapable of fulfilling their obligations, leading to a potential collapse in lending and a sharp economic contraction.

The math is simple:

* **Reduced Domestic Liquidity:** $10.5 billion outflow + global shadow banking crisis = immediate liquidity stress for domestic banks.
* **Dollarized Economy Vulnerability:** 83.4% dollarization amplifies the impact of US dollar scarcity and global financial instability.
* **Frozen Reserves:** Offshore credit vehicles holding reserves create a physical barrier to domestic bank operations.
* **Power Shift:** Offshore lenders control the frozen assets, giving them leverage over the domestic economy.

The result is a self-reinforcing cycle of capital flight, liquidity freeze, and economic contraction.
