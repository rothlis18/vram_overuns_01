A $10.5 billion outflow from private commercial banks into offshore assets, coinciding with a global shadow banking liquidity crisis, creates a direct and severe threat to the domestic economy. 

Here's the breakdown:

* **Chain Reaction:**  The bank capital flight exacerbates the shadow banking liquidity crunch.  As offshore lenders face redemption pressure, they are forced to liquidate assets, further depressing market prices and tightening credit. This creates a feedback loop, driving more capital flight and deepening the crisis.

* **Dollarized Economy Vulnerability:** A 83.4% dollarized economy means domestic institutions and individuals rely heavily on US dollars. When offshore credit vehicles freeze, local depositors cannot access their dollar-denominated reserves. This triggers a panic as individuals attempt to withdraw physical cash, exceeding the banks' immediate liquidity capacity.

* **Liquidity Freeze:** The banks, facing simultaneous capital flight and mass withdrawals, are unable to meet their obligations. This triggers a liquidity freeze, halting lending and economic activity. Businesses cannot access credit, consumers cannot spend, and the economy grinds to a halt.

* **Power Dynamics:** Offshore shadow lenders hold a significant power advantage. They control the flow of capital and can dictate terms to domestic institutions. The legal freezing of reserves within these offshore vehicles effectively removes the domestic government's ability to intervene and protect its citizens.

**Math:**

The $10.5 billion outflow represents a significant portion of the domestic banking system's capital.  The 83.4% dollarization rate indicates the vast majority of domestic financial assets are tied to the US dollar. The resulting liquidity freeze will have a cascading effect on the entire economy, with the severity dependent on the size and interconnectedness of the domestic financial system.


**Conclusion:**

The scenario described represents a classic bank run amplified by global financial instability. The power dynamics favor offshore lenders, leaving the domestic economy vulnerable to a severe and potentially protracted crisis.
