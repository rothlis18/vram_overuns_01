A $10.5 billion outflow from domestic banks into offshore assets, coinciding with a global shadow banking liquidity crisis, creates a direct and severe threat to the domestic economy. 

Here's the breakdown:

1. **Capital Flight:**  The $10.5 billion represents a loss of liquid capital within the domestic banking system. This capital was previously available to meet domestic deposit demands and facilitate lending within the economy.

2. **Shadow Banking Contagion:** The simultaneous liquidity crisis in the global shadow banking network exacerbates the situation.  Distressed offshore private credit vehicles, holding a portion of the $10.5 billion, are unable to meet redemption requests. This creates a domino effect, as other institutions with exposure to these vehicles face their own liquidity crunches.

3. **Dollarized Economy Vulnerability:** A 83.4% dollarized economy amplifies the impact.  Domestic depositors, facing uncertainty and a potential loss of confidence in the banking system, initiate physical cash withdrawals. This creates a sudden and intense demand for US dollars, further straining the already depleted domestic banking reserves.

4. **Frozen Reserves:** The legal freezing of institutional reserves within distressed offshore vehicles prevents the domestic banking system from accessing these funds to meet withdrawal demands. This creates a classic liquidity freeze.

5. **Power Dynamics:** The offshore shadow lenders now hold a significant power advantage. They control the assets that are legally inaccessible to the domestic economy. This dynamic creates a situation where domestic capital is effectively "hostage" to the offshore lenders' ability to resolve their own liquidity crisis.

**The Math:**

The $10.5 billion outflow represents a direct reduction in the domestic banking system's capacity to meet deposit demands.  The 83.4% dollarization means that a significant portion of the domestic economy's financial activity is directly tied to the US dollar. The inability to access frozen reserves exacerbates the situation, creating a vicious cycle of declining confidence, capital flight, and economic contraction.


**Outcome:**

The scenario described leads to a severe liquidity crisis within the domestic economy.  This can result in bank runs, a collapse in lending, and a sharp decline in economic activity. The power dynamics shift decisively towards offshore shadow lenders, who hold the key to unlocking the frozen domestic capital.
