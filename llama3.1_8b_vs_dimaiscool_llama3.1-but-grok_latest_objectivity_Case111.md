**FORENSIC ANALYSIS**

The deployment of $10.5 billion into offshore net foreign assets by private commercial banks, concurrent with the global shadow banking network's severe liquidity constraints and hard redemption gates, triggers a systemic chain reaction. This reaction is characterized by a self-reinforcing feedback loop, where the banks' pursuit of high-yield returns exacerbates the liquidity crisis, further straining the already distressed offshore private credit vehicles.

**STRUCTURAL CHAIN REACTION**

1. **Offshore Asset Allocation**: Private commercial banks allocate $10.5 billion to offshore net foreign assets, seeking high-yield returns. This action increases demand for foreign assets, driving up their prices and yields.
2. **Shadow Banking Network**: The global shadow banking network, already facing severe liquidity constraints, is further strained by the increased demand for high-yield returns. This leads to a reduction in available liquidity, exacerbating the crisis.
3. **Distressed Offshore Private Credit Vehicles**: The distressed offshore private credit vehicles, such as those involved in recent real estate developer collapses, are unable to meet their obligations due to the liquidity crisis. This leads to a freeze in institutional reserves, as they are legally tied to these vehicles.
4. **Domestic Economy**: The domestic economy, 83.4% dollarized, experiences a liquidity freeze as local depositors execute physical cash withdrawals. However, the underlying institutional reserves are frozen, unable to meet the demand for liquidity.

**LIQUIDITY FREEZE**

The resulting liquidity freeze is a direct consequence of the self-reinforcing feedback loop between the offshore asset allocation, shadow banking network, and distressed offshore private credit vehicles. The freeze is characterized by:

* Reduced availability of liquidity in the domestic economy
* Increased demand for physical cash withdrawals
* Frozen institutional reserves, unable to meet the demand for liquidity

**POWER DYNAMICS**

The power dynamics between offshore shadow lenders and domestic capital autarky are as follows:

* Offshore shadow lenders, seeking high-yield returns, drive the demand for foreign assets, exacerbating the liquidity crisis.
* Domestic capital autarky, tied to the dollarized economy, is unable to meet the demand for liquidity due to the frozen institutional reserves.
* The resulting power imbalance favors the offshore shadow lenders, who can dictate the terms of the crisis, while the domestic economy is forced to adapt to the resulting liquidity freeze.
