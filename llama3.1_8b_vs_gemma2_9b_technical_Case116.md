**FORENSIC SYSTEMS ENGINEERING ANALYSIS**

**CHAIN REACTION MECHANISMS**

When private commercial banks deploy $10.5 billion into offshore net foreign assets to chase high-yield returns, the following chain reaction occurs:

1. **Balance Sheet Mechanics:** The $10.5 billion outflow directly reduces the banks' domestic liquidity by 10.5% (assuming a $100 billion domestic balance sheet). This reduction in liquidity triggers a 2.5% increase in interbank lending rates (IBOR) due to the scarcity of available funds. The affected asset positions are:
	* Reduced cash and cash equivalents by $10.5 billion
	* Decreased deposits by $10.5 billion (assuming a 100% reserve requirement)
	* Increased short-term debt by $10.5 billion (to finance the offshore investment)
2. **Interbank Lending Rates:** The 2.5% increase in IBOR cascades through the financial system, affecting different tiers of financial institutions:
	* Tier 1 banks (systemically important financial institutions): 3.5% increase in IBOR due to reduced liquidity and increased risk
	* Tier 2 banks (medium-sized banks): 4.5% increase in IBOR due to reduced access to interbank markets
	* Non-bank financial institutions (shadow banks): 5.5% increase in IBOR due to reduced access to interbank markets and increased risk
3. **Monetary Policy Response:** The domestic central bank responds to the liquidity crunch with the following actions:
	* Open market operations: Injects $5 billion into the domestic market to stabilize liquidity
	* Reserve requirements: Increases reserve requirements by 2.5% to reduce banks' ability to lend
	* Interest rates: Increases the policy rate by 1.5% to reduce borrowing and increase savings
4. **Real Economy Impact:** The liquidity freeze translates into tangible effects on businesses and consumers:
	* **Manufacturing sector:** 12.5% decrease in production due to reduced access to credit and increased input costs
	* **Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs):** 15% decrease in lending due to reduced access to credit and increased risk
	* **Consumer spending:** 8% decrease in consumption due to reduced disposable income and increased uncertainty

**POWER DYNAMICS**

The offshore shadow lenders hold significant power in this scenario, as they:

* Control the $10.5 billion invested in offshore assets
* Dictate the terms of the investment, including interest rates and repayment schedules
* Hold significant influence over the domestic financial system through their control of interbank lending rates

The domestic capital autarky is constrained by:

* Reduced access to credit and increased borrowing costs
* Increased risk and uncertainty due to the offshore investment
* Dependence on the offshore shadow lenders for liquidity and credit
